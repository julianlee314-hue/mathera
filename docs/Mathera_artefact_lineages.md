# Mathera artefact lineages

Working cabinet, October 2026. Not a lesson layer. Each object is gained once, then evolves only when a later skill changes its job. Triggers are level 4 of a named skill unless marked otherwise. Grey the next form until that skill is proven.

Frequency is era-span, not a fake hit count. The concept codex never counts occurrences, and the two curriculum files disagree (guide: 1,132 skills, 87 branches; map: 1,080 skills, Era II cut to 6 domains and 83 skills). Spans below follow the codex era chips plus the skill names in the guide.

## Rules

- One living lineage per domain at launch. Side objects only from Brave or Legend.
- Gain = first skill that makes the object usable. Evolve = a later skill that gives it a new job.
- Review polishes the current form. It does not evolve it.
- One sentence on the object: the job, not the history. A year only if it teaches the job.

## Lineages

### 1. Tally stick
Concepts: Counting, Cardinality, Number Representation. Span: I–II, cardinality fades after II.
- Notch stick — I.1.01 Count objects one by one. One notch, one thing.
- Bundle stick — I.3.01 Ten ones make a ten. Ten notches tied as one bundle.
- Numeral tablet — I.1.06 Read and write numerals to 20. Marks replaced by a sign.
- Hundred chart — I.3.11 The hundred chart. The tablet becomes a field you can move on.

### 2. Ten-frame
Concepts: Counting, Cardinality, Addition. Span: I, then absorbed by place value.
- Five-frame — I.1.09 See amounts to 5 at a glance.
- Ten-frame — I.1.10 See amounts to 10 at a glance.
- Double ten-frame — I.4.10 Make ten. Two frames, fill one first.

### 3. Balance
Concepts: Equality, Equation Solving, Inequality Solving, Inverse Operations. Span: I–VII. Mythic in the codex; this is the long tool.
- Pan balance — I.4.18 The equal sign as a balance. Same weight, both sides.
- Missing-weight balance — I.4.17 Missing addends. One pan covered.
- Letter balance — III.6 first equation skill. A letter stands for the covered weight.
- Inequality beam — III.6 inequality skill. The beam may tip; the solution is a range.
- System of pans — IV.4 Systems. Two balances, shared weights.
- Matrix ledger — VII.2 Systems as matrices. The pans become a grid of coefficients.

### 4. Coin strip
Concepts: Place Value, Addition, Decimals, Percent. Span: I–III.
- Coin row — I.7.11 Coins.
- Make-amount purse — I.7.12 Make amounts.
- Decimal coins — II.7 decimals. A coin is a tenth or a hundredth.
- Percent wheel — III.3 Percent. The purse becomes a wheel of 100.

### 5. Multiplication strip
Concepts: Multiplication, Repeated Operations, Distributivity, Algorithms. Span: I–VII.
- Pair counter — I.1.16 Skip count by 2s.
- Array board — II.2.02 Arrays.
- Fact table — II.2.06 All facts to 10 × 10.
- Area model — II.2.07 The area model. The table splits into rectangles.
- Napier's rods — II.2.13 Multi-digit multiplication. Rods replace the long product. (Napier's bones, 1617, are for products, not logs.)
- Log page — IV.12 first log skill. Products become sums. Napier 1614, Briggs base-10 in 1617.
- Slide rule — IV.12 skill on reading a log scale. Gunter's scale 1620, sliding pair by about 1630. Lengths add.
- Machine key — VI or VII numerical skill, optional. The key hides the page; the learner still has to know when the answer is nonsense.

### 6. Fraction bar
Concepts: Fractions, Equivalence, Division. Span: I–IV.
- Half tile — I.6.11 Halves.
- Equal-parts bar — II.5.01 Equal parts.
- Equivalence strips — II.5.07 Equivalent fractions with models. Same length, different cuts.
- Operator bar — II.5.04 Fractions of a set. The bar acts on a group.
- Division bar — II.5.14 Fractions as division.

### 7. Number line
Concepts: Ordering, Magnitude, Signed Quantity, Coordinates. Span: I–VII.
- Line 0–10 — I.2.03.
- Line 0–20 — I.2.05.
- Open line — I.2.09 Compare to 100.
- Signed line — III.1 Integers. Zero is a place, not the start.
- Rational line — III.1 rationals. Cuts between the integers.
- Real line — IV.11 or IV.12, when gaps and limits of decimals matter.
- Complex plane — IV.8.08 Complex plane. The line grows a second axis. New object, evolved from the line, not a new lineage.

### 8. Clock
Concepts: Measurement, Units, Circular measure, Periodicity. Span: I–V.
- Hour clock — I.7.07 Time to the hour.
- Half-hour clock — I.7.08.
- Minute clock — later measure skill if present; otherwise II measurement.
- Circle clock — V.6 or V.9, when the turn becomes an angle.
- Unit-circle clock — V.9 Trig functions. The hand is a point on the circle.

### 9. Ruler
Concepts: Measurement, Units, Distance, Precision & Error. Span: I–VII.
- Hand span — I.7.03 Nonstandard units.
- Marked ruler — I.7.04 Measure with a ruler.
- Broken ruler — a level-3 variant of the same skill. Zero is not at the end.
- Decimal ruler — II.7.
- Scale ruler — III.2 rates, map scale.
- Error bar — VI.11 inference, or II rounding. The mark is a range.

### 10. Data marks
Concepts: Data Representation, Data Collection, Tables, Graphs, Center, Spread. Span: I–VI.
- Tally gate — I.7.13 Tally marks.
- Picture graph — I.7.14.
- Bar graph — I.7.15.
- Table — II.10 Data.
- Mean stack — III.9 or IV.15 Summary statistics. Bars rearranged to a level.
- Spread marks — IV.15. The level is not enough; the width matters.
- Sample frame — VI.11. The graph is of a sample, not the whole pile.

### 11. Shape tiles
Concepts: Shape, Classification, Symmetry, Congruence, Transformation. Span: I–V.
- Name cards — I.6.01 Name flat shapes.
- Feature sieve — I.6.02 Shape features.
- Mirror — I.6.13 Symmetry.
- Congruent pair — V.2 or V.3, when sameness is proved by moving.
- Stamp set — V.13 Transformations. Turn, flip, slide as three stamps.
- Projection glass — VII.8 projection. A stamp that drops a shadow.

### 12. Grid
Concepts: Position, Coordinates, Spatial Reasoning. Span: I–VII.
- Word grid — I.6.10 Directions on a grid.
- First quadrant — III.7 lines.
- Full plane — III.1 with negatives, or IV.3.
- Vector grid — VII.1. Arrows live on the grid.
- Change-of-basis grid — VII.4. The lines tilt; the point did not move.

### 13. Angle tool
Concepts: Angle, Parallel & Perpendicular, Trigonometric Ratio. Span: I–V.
- Corner checker — III.8 right angle.
- Protractor — III.8 measuring angles.
- Parallel stamp — III.8 or V.2. A tool that copies a direction.
- Right-triangle trig card — V.6. Three ratios on one card.
- General triangle card — V.7 sine and cosine rules.

### 14. Compass and straightedge
Concepts: Geometric Locus, Proof, Construction. Span: V, previewed by shape.
- Straightedge — V.15 first construction.
- Compass — V.15 circle construction.
- Locus pen — V or V.8. The compass draws every point at a fixed distance.
- Proof tablet — V.1. The construction writes its reason beside the mark.

### 15. Formula card
Concepts: Expressions, Variables, Substitution, Rearranging Formulae. Span: II–VII.
- Box card — II.8 Expressions. A box for a number.
- Letter card — III.5. The box is named.
- Rearrange card — III, rearrange formulae. The card can be turned over.
- Family card — IV.9 parameters. One card, a slider.

### 16. Growth vial
Concepts: Powers, Growth & Decay, Exponential, Rate of Change. Span: III–VI.
- Doubling jar — III.4 exponents, or I.5 growing patterns.
- Percent-change jar — III.3.
- Exponential vial — IV.12. The step is a multiply, not an add.
- Decay vial — IV.12 decay. Same glass, the level falls.
- Differential vial — VII.15 or VI.7. The rule is about the rate, not the level.

### 17. Series chain
Concepts: Sequence, Series, Recurrence, Infinite Process. Span: I–VI.
- Bead chain — I.5.05 Number patterns.
- Arithmetic chain — IV.13.
- Geometric chain — IV.13.
- Infinite chain — VI.9. The chain does not finish; the sum may.
- Taylor chain — VI.10. A curve copied by a finite piece of the chain.

### 18. Chance bowl
Concepts: Counting Outcomes, Probability, Independence, Conditional Probability, Expected Value. Span: III–VI.
- Two-color bowl — III.9.
- Outcome tree — IV.14 counting.
- Independence pair — IV.14. Two bowls, neither cares.
- Conditional bowl — IV.14. One color already drawn.
- Expectation purse — IV.14 or VI. The bowl pays a value, not just a color.

### 19. Slope glass
Concepts: Slope, Rate of Change, Derivative, Local Linearization. Span: III–VII.
- Rise-run card — III.7.
- Secant glass — VI.1 limits. Two points, a chord.
- Tangent glass — VI.2 Derivatives. The chord shrinks to a touch.
- Linear lens — VI.2 or VI.3 local linearization. Near the touch, the curve is the glass.
- Gradient lens — VII.11. The glass has a direction on a surface.

### 20. Area sweep
Concepts: Area, Accumulation, Integral, Fundamental Theorem. Span: I–VII.
- Tile fill — II.9 area.
- Strip estimate — VI.4 integrals.
- Accumulation cup — VI.4. Area poured into a running total.
- FTC link — VI.4 fundamental theorem skill. The cup's rate is the height of the strip.
- Volume sweep — VI.6 or VII.13. The strip becomes a slab.

### 21. Vector arrow
Concepts: Vector, Scalar combination, Span, Independence, Basis, Projection. Span: V preview, VII main. Codex caps these at Tier V because the course ends; the objects should not look common.
- Arrow — VII.1.
- Sum arrow — VII.1 addition of vectors. Tip to tail.
- Span fan — VII.4. All arrows you can mix from a given set.
- Basis frame — VII.4. A smallest fan that still fills the plane.
- Shadow arrow — VII.8 projection. The part that lands in the frame.

### 22. Matrix press
Concepts: Matrix, Linear Transformation, Determinant, Eigenstructure. Span: IV.4 preview, VII.
- Coefficient grid — IV.4.15 or VII.2.
- Press — VII.5. The grid is a machine that moves arrows.
- Area stamp — VII.6 determinant. The press reports the signed area.
- Eigen axis — VII.7. Directions the press only stretches.
- SVD stack — VII.9. Any press is a turn, a stretch, a turn.

### 23. Model bench
Concepts: Mathematical Modelling, Constraints, Optimization. Span: I–VII.
- Story card — I.4.03 Addition stories.
- Constraint frame — III.6 or IV.1. The story has a fence.
- Fit bench — VII.8 least squares. The line is the closest, not the one through every point.
- Optimizer — VI.3 or VII.12. The bench finds a high or low point.

### 24. Proof seal
Concepts: Logical Statements, Implication, Counterexample, Deduction, Necessary & Sufficient, Proof. Span: I–V.
- True-false token — I.5.10.
- If-then card — I.5.12.
- Counterexample stone — V.1. One case breaks a claim.
- Seal — V.1 deduction. The claim is closed.
- Construction seal — V.15. The picture is the proof.

### 25. Error bead
Concepts: Precision & Error, Estimation, Approximation by Refinement. Span: I–VI.
- Guess bead — I.2.11 Estimate a quantity.
- Round bead — II.1.05 Round to tens and hundreds.
- Remainder bead — II.3.06. Something left over, kept on purpose.
- Refinement bead — VI.1 or VI.4. Smaller steps, closer answer.
- Interval bead — VI.11. The answer is a span.
